Step 1: Scenario Hook & Warm-up (5-8 min)

Describing Other People's Routines (Unit 1)

When talking about **he, she, or it**, regular verbs in affirmative sentences simply take an -s. Negatives use doesn't and questions use Does. Let's explore how professional habits shape daily routines.

NARRATIVE CONTEXT · LUCAS & MATEUS

Step 3: Grammar Calibration (10-12 min)

Grammar Focus: Unit 1 (The Simple -s Rule, Doesn't & Does)

Let's master how verbs take **-s** in affirmative sentences, how **doesn't** builds negatives, and how **Does** forms questions:

1. Affirmative (+) [Verb + -s]

Describing habits:

• He works from home.

• She starts early.

• He loves his job.

2. Negative (-) [Doesn't + verb]

Denying routines:

• He doesn't work in an office.

• She doesn't like traffic.

• He doesn't stop early.

3. Interrogative (?) [Does + subject]

Asking questions:

• Does he work remotely?

• Does she like coffee?

• Does he live downtown?

Fossilized Error Guard (Precision Check)
❌ COMMON MISTAKE

"He doesn't works on weekends." (double verb inflection)

"Does she works here?" (adding -s after 'does')

🟢 CORRECT STRUCTURE

"He doesn't work on weekends."

"Does she work here?"
